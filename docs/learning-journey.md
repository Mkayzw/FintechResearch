# Fieldlab Learning Journey

## Objective

Each Fieldlab module turns one ZIMSEC Physics syllabus area into a complete learning cycle rather than a disconnected simulation. A learner should move from curiosity to prediction, model building, examination practice and independent investigation while always knowing what is core, derived or an extension.

## Canonical journey

1. **Orient** — show the provisional syllabus objective, prerequisites, assumptions and success criteria.
2. **Observe** — present the phenomenon before the derivation and invite careful noticing.
3. **Predict** — require a falsifiable estimate or choice before revealing the model result.
4. **Explore** — manipulate one variable at a time and inspect synchronized physical and graphical representations.
5. **Derive** — build the equations from definitions and coordinate conventions; never present an unexplained formula wall.
6. **Apply** — work through scaffolded calculations, then remove scaffolding.
7. **Investigate** — plan or conduct a practical with variables, repeat readings, uncertainty, graphing and evaluation.
8. **Practise** — train the distinct demands of multiple-choice, structured, free-response and practical questions.
9. **Review** — expose misconceptions, annotate where marks are earned and prompt correction.
10. **Extend** — critique the core model through a clearly labelled research extension.
11. **Connect** — state what prior knowledge was used and identify the next curriculum destination.

## Interaction contract

- A visible route map tells the learner where they are going.
- Every interaction has a learning purpose, not merely animation value.
- Prediction happens before the answer is emphasized.
- Physical animation, equations, vectors, plots and readouts share one state.
- Controls use SI units and expose assumptions.
- Feedback explains why an answer works or fails.
- Practical work distinguishes measured values, uncertainty and model assumptions.
- Research extensions are visually and verbally separated from examinable core.
- No account or saved-progress system is required; progress is session-local and learner-controlled.

## Examination contract

Each core module should eventually contain:

- a misconception-driven multiple-choice set;
- at least one scaffolded structured calculation;
- at least one extended explanation or derivation;
- a practical planning/data-analysis task;
- a marking guide that identifies method, substitution, units and interpretation without pretending to reproduce an unavailable official mark scheme;
- a “repair your answer” step after feedback.

## Projectile Motion mapping

| Journey stage | Current experience |
|---|---|
| Orient | Syllabus briefing and learning outcomes |
| Observe | Live launch stage and synchronized plots |
| Predict | Movable range marker before release |
| Explore | Speed, angle, height, timeline and vector controls |
| Derive | Guided vector, kinematics, apex and range sequence |
| Apply | Range-angle experiment and structured worked problem |
| Investigate | Repeat-reading table and uncertainty calculator |
| Practise | Multiple-choice concept check and exam bench |
| Review | Immediate explanations, method guide and common-error prompts |
| Extend | Explicitly labelled quadratic-drag comparison |
| Connect | Mechanics route back to the curriculum map and forward to advanced nonlinear motion |

## Success criteria

- A learner can identify the examinable model before touching an extension.
- A learner can explain why horizontal and vertical motion may be treated independently while sharing time.
- A learner can solve an elevated-launch problem with a declared sign convention and positive impact root.
- A learner can state when the $45^\circ$ result is valid and when it is not.
- A learner can design a repeatable investigation and report uncertainty honestly.
- A learner encounters all four provisional public-examination modes in the module.
- Keyboard and reduced-motion users can complete the same conceptual journey.
