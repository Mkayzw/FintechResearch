## Verified provisional curriculum scope

The accessible mirror places these areas under Newtonian Mechanics:

1. Kinematics
2. Dynamics
3. Forces
4. Work, Energy and Power
5. Circular Motion
6. Gravitational Field

For projectile motion, its curriculum matrix explicitly expects learners to:

- derive uniformly accelerated straight-line equations from velocity and acceleration;
- solve uniformly accelerated motion problems, including free fall in a uniform gravitational field without air resistance;
- describe and explain motion produced by uniform velocity in one direction and uniform acceleration in a perpendicular direction;
- solve problems using standard projectile-motion equations;
- identify and explain everyday examples of rectilinear and non-linear motion.

Supporting General Physics skills include:

- resolving vectors into perpendicular components;
- using SI units and checking dimensional homogeneity;
- translating among graphical, numerical, algebraic and verbal forms;
- selecting graph variables and scales, drawing best-fit trends and interpreting gradients, intercepts and areas;
- distinguishing systematic and random errors, precision and accuracy;
- combining absolute, fractional and percentage uncertainties.

## Content classification

Every lesson must visibly distinguish these layers.

### Examinable core — provisional

- resolve initial velocity into horizontal and vertical components;
- use $a_x=0$ and $a_y=-g$ with a stated coordinate convention;
- apply constant-acceleration equations independently in each direction;
- describe and explain the shared-time model;
- solve projectile problems without air resistance;
- interpret motion graphs, units, measurements and uncertainty.

### Derived core application

These are standard consequences of the core model and reasonable applications of “standard equations,” but were not individually listed as syllabus objectives in the accessible matrix:

- time of flight;
- maximum height and the apex condition $v_y=0$;
- horizontal range;
- impact speed and direction;
- complementary-angle and $45^\circ$ results when launch and landing heights are equal.

### Research extension — not verified as examinable

- quadratic drag and drag coefficients;
- numerical RK4 integration and impact interpolation;
- wind and atmospheric modelling;
- elevated-launch range optimisation beyond standard exercises;
- double-pendulum chaos and finite-time divergence.

Extensions must deepen model criticism without displacing the vacuum model students need for examinations.

## Provisional assessment model

The accessible mirror gives the following structure. It must be rechecked against the official syllabus before being represented as final.

| Paper | Mode | Raw marks | Duration | Weight | Product preparation |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | Multiple choice | 40 | 1 hour | 15% | Concept discrimination, units, signs, graphs and common distractors |
| 2 | Structured | 60 | 1 hour 30 minutes | 20% | Short derivations and linked calculations with visible method |
| 3 | Free response | 100 | 2 hours 30 minutes | 30% | Extended Mechanics reasoning; the mirror describes General Physics and Newtonian Mechanics as the compulsory question area |
| 4 | Practical | 50 | 2 hours 30 minutes | 15% | Apparatus, tables, graphs, uncertainty, analysis, evaluation and design |
| 5 | School-based project | 50 | During the school year | 20% | Problem identification, investigation, solution generation, refinement, presentation and evaluation |

No current official question-level mark scheme or command-word glossary was accessible. The application may coach transparent scientific working, but must not claim unverified rules for error-carried-forward, significant-figure penalties, units penalties or exact mark allocation.

## Exam-answer discipline

The curriculum uses assessable verbs including **state**, **describe**, **explain**, **derive**, **calculate**, **solve**, **analyse**, **interpret**, **determine**, **design** and **evaluate**. Fieldlab uses the following general coaching, without presenting it as an official mark scheme:

- **State:** give the requested fact directly.
- **Describe:** report the relevant pattern or behaviour.
- **Explain:** connect the observation to a physical principle using cause and effect.
- **Derive:** begin from accepted relations, show algebraic steps and state assumptions.
- **Calculate/solve:** define the coordinate convention, select equations, substitute SI values, retain units and interpret the result.
- **Analyse:** extract a relationship from data or graphs and support it with evidence.
- **Design:** identify variables, apparatus, method, controls, safety and analysis.
- **Evaluate:** identify a specific limitation, its effect and a realistic improvement.

## Projectile module evidence matrix

| Learning outcome | Interactive evidence | Examination evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Resolve a launch vector | Toggle vectors and vary speed/angle | Component calculation with signs and units |
| Explain independent motions | Scrub synchronized trajectory and plots | Written explanation using $a_x=0$, $a_y=-g$ and common time |
| Solve standard problems | Read apex, range, duration and impact state | Structured calculation with equation selection and interpretation |
| Test the $45^\circ$ condition | Generate range-versus-angle evidence | State the equal-height, no-drag assumptions before using $R=u^2\sin2\theta/g$ |
| Plan and analyse an investigation | Edit repeat trials and uncertainty | Variables, table headings, graph, uncertainty and evaluation |
| Critique a model | Compare vacuum with labelled drag extension | Distinguish an examinable idealisation from a numerical extension |
